What is
malware?

Malware is bad software designed to harm or spy on your computer or device.

It acts like a digital germ that can make your device sick or slow.

Definition: Malware comes from the words “malicious software.” It is any program made to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to a device or its data.

Viruses – Spreading the Infection

Needs a file to spread

Viruses attach to files and replicate when those files are shared.

They spread through infected downloads and unsafe attachments.

Watch for slow computers, strange errors, and missing files.

Definition: A virus is code that attaches itself to a legitimate file or program and copies itself when that file is opened or shared.

Worms – The Fast Movers

Spreads on its own

Worms spread on their own across networks without files.

They travel through the internet, shared networks, and emails.

Watch for slow internet, repeated pop-ups, and computer crashes.

Definition: A worm is self-copying malware that moves from device to device over a network without needing a host file.

Trojan Horses – Hidden Danger

Looks harmless

A Trojan Horse looks harmless but hides harmful software.

It spreads through fake games, apps, or files from untrusted sources.

Watch for new programs you didn’t install, unexpected ads, or strange behavior.

Definition: A Trojan is malware disguised as a useful or fun program to trick you into installing it.

Spyware – The Silent Spy

Spyware secretly watches what you do and sends information about you to someone else, usually without you ever noticing.

Definition: Spyware is malware that hides on a device and collects personal information, such as passwords, browsing habits, messages, or what you type, then sends it to a third party without your permission.
🕵️
spy_log.txt (a pretend example, not real data)
> visited: shopping-site.example
> typed: ************ (password)
> clipboard copied: message...
> sending data to stranger...
Spyware works quietly in the background, which is why you often can’t see it.
🔎 What it does
  • Tracks the websites you visit
  • Records what you type (passwords, messages)
  • Collects personal info and account details
  • May show ads or change your browser settings
🚪 How it spreads
  • Bundled inside free software or fake apps
  • Clicking unsafe links or pop-up ads
  • Infected email attachments
  • Downloads from untrusted websites
🚨 Warning signs
  • Device is slow or the battery drains fast
  • New toolbars, homepage, or search engine
  • Pop-ups appear even when not browsing
  • Unusual data use or accounts acting strangely

Common kinds of spyware

⌨️ Keylogger

Records every key you press to capture passwords and messages.

📢 Adware

Tracks your habits and floods you with unwanted ads.

🍪 Tracking cookies

Small files that follow your activity across websites.

📱 Stalkerware

Apps secretly installed on a phone to monitor someone’s activity.

Recap

Watch out for these warning signs

  • ⏱ Slow or crashing device
  • ⚠ Strange error messages or pop-ups
  • 📄 Files disappearing or changing
  • 🖥 Programs or ads you didn’t expect
  • ⚙ Unexpected changes to settings or browser
  • 👁 Feeling watched: odd logins, unusual data use, battery draining fast

How to prevent malware & spyware

Good habits are your best protection. Tick the ones you already follow and watch your safety meter grow!

🛡️ Your safety meter 0 / 9 habits
Start ticking the boxes below.
1

Install security software

Use a trusted antivirus / anti-malware program and run regular scans.

2

Keep everything updated

Update your operating system, browser, and apps. Updates fix security holes.

3

Download only from official sources

Use official app stores and real websites of software makers. Avoid cracked or “free” versions of paid games.

4

Think before you click

Don’t open unknown attachments or click suspicious links or pop-ups, even if they look exciting.

5

Use strong, unique passwords

Turn on two-factor authentication so a stolen password alone isn’t enough.

6

Check app permissions

Does a flashlight app need your contacts or microphone? If not, deny it.

7

Back up your files

Keep copies on a separate drive or cloud storage so you can recover if something goes wrong.

8

Be careful on public Wi-Fi

Avoid logging in to important accounts on open networks, or use a trusted VPN.

9

Tell a trusted adult

If something feels wrong, stop and ask a parent, teacher, or IT person for help.

Think you’re infected? Disconnect from the internet, run a full scan with your security software, uninstall apps you don’t recognize, change your passwords from a clean device, and ask a trusted adult for help.
